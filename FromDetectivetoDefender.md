<h1 align="center">🛡️ From Detective to Defender: How One Conversation Sparked My Cybersecurity Career</h1>


![cyber-security-concept-shield-with-keyhole-icon-vector](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/1934d229-cfb4-4977-b209-da4b2439d3e1)


My journey began when I signed up for the **NYPD police academy** back in **2015**. I spent eight years with the NYPD, first on patrol responding to everything from serious crimes in progress to calls for help, crisis situations, and non-criminal matters where people simply needed someone to show up. Later, I was selected to join the **Domestic Violence Unit**, where I served as a **detective for five years**, managing a caseload of over **1,000 active cases**. My work involved conducting detailed follow-ups, writing **DD5s** in our database system, locating and apprehending offenders, and creatively gathering intelligence on their whereabouts.  
(*One of my favorite moments: after learning from victims where several offenders got their haircuts, I ended up arresting them right in the barber’s chair — talk about thinking a step ahead!*)

One quiet night during my shift, a coworker — not someone I knew very well at the time (**though we’re great friends now**) — mentioned in passing that he was studying for the **CompTIA Security+** exam. That caught my attention. As we started talking, he mentioned his wife was already working in the field and described cybersecurity as **“the digital world of policing.”** That line stuck with me.

It clicked immediately. The same instincts that helped me track down offenders or connect the dots across **1,000+ investigations** were the very skills used to hunt **digital adversaries**. The deeper we talked, the more I saw the overlap: **investigations, pattern recognition, protecting people, and staying ahead of threats** — just in a different landscape.

I realized many of those same instincts applied. In **law enforcement**, especially during investigations or active crimes in progress, I had to **think like the suspect** — anticipate their next move, notice inconsistencies, and dig into small details others might overlook. That same mindset translates directly into **threat hunting** and **incident response** in cybersecurity.

That conversation planted a seed. I started exploring the field and began with the **fundamentals**, eventually studying for and passing **Security+**. I was hooked. From there, I enrolled in a **cybersecurity degree program**, began stacking **certifications**, and fully dove into **Blue Team** work.

Today, I’m working toward my **Bachelor’s in Cybersecurity** (*graduating December 2025*) and interning with **Log(N) Pacific**, where I support:

- **Threat hunting** and **alert triage** in *Microsoft Defender XDR*  
- Writing **KQL queries** to detect suspicious behavior  
- Remediating **STIG findings** and **hardening Windows endpoints**  
- Mapping detections to the **MITRE ATT&CK framework**  

I’ve earned certifications including **CompTIA Security+**, **Microsoft Azure Fundamentals**, and the **TCM Security SOC Analyst** (*hands-on*) — and I continue to grow through **hands-on labs**, **personal projects**, and **writing about what I’m learning** (many of which I document on my [GitHub](https://github.com/GScalzo21)).

This space is where I document the journey — from the **street to the SOC**. If you’re transitioning careers, building detection skills, or just passionate about **defense**, I hope you find something here that helps or inspires you.

**Let’s hunt some threats.**
