# Navigating the Cloud: A Newcomer's Journey with Azure and AWS.

# Cloud-y With a Chance of Beanstalks?

Jumping into cloud computing as someone changing careers felt like stepping off the curb and straight into a tech supercity — bright lights, endless dashboards, and more acronyms than street signs. When I first started exploring the cloud, I was both pumped and a little lost. 

My journey kicked off with **Azure** through *Josh Madakor’s Cybersecurity Course*, and honestly, it felt like I had a legit tour guide. Everything was laid out, and for once, the path didn’t feel like a maze.

---

## First Impressions: Azure, the Sophisticated Architect

Azure was my first taste of cloud computing, and it made a **solid first impression**. Opening the portal felt oddly familiar — clean, simple, and intuitive, like other Microsoft tools I’ve used over the years.

It was like being walked through a modern skyscraper by a professional architect who actually knows what they’re doing — confident, structured, and surprisingly easy to follow.

What stood out to me was how organized it all felt. I’d click around, dive into one service, back out, go deeper into another, and never felt like I was getting lost. The layout just makes sense.

> 💡 And let’s be real — that little ‘X’ in the top corner to exit a section instead of having to mash the back button like a caveman?  
> **Small win. Big relief.**

Another thing I appreciated: Azure doesn’t try to play guessing games.

- **Virtual Machines** are called *Virtual Machines*  
- **Storage** is *Blob Storage*  
- No secret code names. No guessing. No stress.

Everything is clearly labeled and feels approachable.

Plus, the integration with other Microsoft tools like **Active Directory** (now rebranded as **EntraID**) makes it feel like one cohesive suite instead of a bunch of services duct-taped together.

Of course, Azure isn’t flawless.

One thing you learn quickly is that **Microsoft loves renaming things**. Just when you finally remember what something’s called — boom, it changes.

> *“Azure is pretty easy to pick up and use, but Microsoft sure loves to keep us on our toes with constant product name changes—just when you finally remember what something's called, they rename it!”*  
> — Joshua Olivo, The Cyber Range Community

And yeah — sometimes the Azure portal needs a quick refresh when it gets moody. But that’s part of the charm.

Like any good tool, you start to learn its personality — the good, the weird, and the “why is this doing that today?” moments.


Even with its quirks, **Azure’s been a great first stop** in my cloud journey. 

It’s user-friendly, it’s familiar, and it makes the cloud feel less like a buzzword and more like a practical tool I can actually use.

➡️ **AWS is next on my radar — but Azure? That’s home base for now.**

---

## Enter AWS: The "Figure It Out as You Go" Cloud

After getting comfortable in Azure, I knew I couldn’t just stop there. You hear it everywhere in this field — **multi-cloud experience** is the way to go. So I decided it was time to dive into **AWS**. 

I didn’t do a formal bootcamp, but I started working through **AWS Educate** to build up a solid foundation and get familiar with the platform.

Right from the start, AWS felt different. If Azure was the polished architect, AWS was more like being handed a massive toolbox and told:  

> *“Here you go — build whatever you want.”*

No tour guide, no clear starting point — just raw power and possibilities.

When I first opened the **AWS Management Console**, it didn’t exactly roll out the red carpet. The interface felt busy, older, and honestly a little overwhelming compared to Azure’s cleaner design. 

And then came the service names: **EC2**, **S3**, **Lambda**, **Redshift**…  
It was like trying to learn a new language while building at the same time.

AWS definitely gives you a ton of flexibility and power — no question about that. But it also expects you to do the legwork. I found myself constantly jumping between docs and the console, trying to piece things together. 

I missed the simple, logical layout I’d gotten used to in Azure.

---

## 🧗 Learning Curve, but Making Progress

I’m still working through **AWS Educate** and preparing for the **AWS Cloud Practitioner exam**.

I haven’t taken it yet, but the fundamentals are starting to click:

- Spinning up **EC2 instances**  
- Understanding different **storage options**  
- Getting a feel for **IAM roles and policies**  
- Experimenting with **Elastic Load Balancers** and learning how AWS pieces fit together

The more I explore AWS, the more I realize just how deep this ecosystem runs.  

It’s a platform that gives you a huge amount of flexibility — but with that flexibility comes complexity.

There’s no sugarcoating it — AWS takes more work to learn. The naming conventions alone took some getting used to, and the console can feel like a dense forest at times compared to **Azure’s** more polished layout.  

I often find myself flipping between AWS docs and the console just to make sure I’m headed in the right direction.

---

## 🏠 The Verdict: Why Azure Still Feels Like Home (For Now)

Despite gaining a new respect for **AWS**, **Azure still feels like home** at this stage of my cloud journey.

And it’s not just about familiarity — it’s about the experience.  

Azure’s intuitive layout and clear product names help me focus on building and learning, instead of constantly trying to decode what service does what.

Because I’m also spending a lot of time with tools like:

- **Microsoft Defender XDR**  
- **Microsoft Sentinel**  
- **Entra ID / Azure Active Directory**

…it just makes sense to operate in an environment where all these tools feel connected.  

That cohesion matters when you’re still building your cloud skills.

Yes, I definitely appreciate **AWS’s** power and flexibility. There’s no denying that it offers a huge array of tools and a global reach.  

But for someone who’s still finding their footing in this space, **Azure’s** smoother user experience gives me the clarity and confidence I need right now.

---

## 🚀 Looking Forward: An Evolving Journey

That said — I’m not stopping here. **AWS** is absolutely a platform I plan to keep learning.  

I want to be comfortable in both **Azure** and **AWS**, because being multi-cloud is becoming the norm in this field.

I’ll continue working through **AWS Educate** and get ready to sit for the **Cloud Practitioner exam**.  

And after that? I’ll keep pushing deeper, because there’s no shortage of things to explore in **AWS** once you get past the surface.

As for **Google Cloud** — I haven’t touched it yet. That chapter is still waiting to be written.

But that’s the fun part of this journey — there’s always something new to learn. And right now, I’m enjoying every step of it.
